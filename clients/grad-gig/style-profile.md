## What each reference brings

**@joshuablatch: the editorial, "designed" look** `[research]`
- Feels like a **magazine page, not a template.** Serif headlines with one italic
  accent word in colour ("Make it *yours*"), small mono labels, numbered
  sections ("01 The library", "02 The rule").
- **Two caption styles working together:** bold uppercase sans with **one keyword
  in a yellow highlight** ("I DESTROYED MY **HIP**"), plus quiet lowercase
  serif single words for the rest.
- **Graphics tied to real things**: a real scan, a real body, real
  screenshots, not generic stock.
- **Words "in the room":** text placed on the wall behind him so it looks
  physically there, not floating on top.
- **One colour grade on every take**, so different filming days match.
- **A look per series:** each recurring series has its own look, with a
  shared brand thread.
- His *process* is the one we're building: a graphics library, plus Claude
  pitching missing graphics ("ideas only, Josh picks"), plus a house caption style.

**@kallawaymarketing: explainer motion graphics** `[research]`
- Split screen (graphic top, speaker card bottom) alternating with full-face
  punch-ins, a new visual every 1–2s, colour-coded concepts, diagrams instead of
  b-roll, 1–2-word captions. Full breakdown in the Kallaway guide.

## Proposed Grad Gig look `[AI guess, for Tait to approve]`
1. **Base:** Luke (and students) talking to camera, one warm, bright colour
   grade (Wheaton, friendly, trustworthy for parents).
2. **Captions:** the Blatch two-style system in Grad Gig colours. Bold sans
   with one highlighted keyword per line (the money, the job, the place:
   "**$45** AN HOUR", "**WHEATON**"), plus quiet serif single words.
3. **Headlines:** serif plus one italic accent word ("Hire a *student*").
4. **Explainers** (how Grad Gig works, pricing, safety): Kallaway-style split
   screen with simple diagrams (post → apply → approve → done → paid
   automatically). No claims of background checks (see brain.md).
5. **Real things on screen:** real gigs, real students, the real app, real
   Wheaton places, client assets from Drive, never stock.
6. **Text in the room** on some shots, as Blatch does (e.g. "$40/hr" on the wall
   behind a student).
7. **Series looks** (to decide in Step 1): e.g. "Student of the week",
   "Before → after" (job footage), "Ask Luke" (parents' questions).

## What viewers say `[research]`
From comments on the biggest videos:
- They **root for young workers**: "Love to see young people working to make some money!", "Great job, young man."
- They **debate price**: "How much do you charge", "You have to start charging more!!"
- They **want the result**: "Why do you not show before and after shots?" (29 likes); "Pls show videos that show finished product" (63).
- They ask to **hire / work**: "Are you hiring?"

## Hook shapes that work here `[research → for Grad Gig]`
1. **Day + goal:** "Day 3 of following a Wheaton student to $5,000 this semester."
2. **Conflict line:** "[Someone] said this job was too small." / "Every company in town said no."
3. **Number + age/identity:** "This Wheaton College sophomore made $[X] doing [gig] this fall." (from the "15-year-old built a 6-figure business" shape)
4. **Customer's own question** (from customer-data.md, `[client's words]`): "'Is this job too small?'", "'Are they actually Wheaton students?'", "'Can we have the same student back next time?'", "'I don't know who else to call.'"
